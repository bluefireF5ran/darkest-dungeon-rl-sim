## 2026-10-11 (late): the seated party now keeps the quest's level rule

`embarkplan.pick_quest` puts a quest in reach when the roster's four best
rested heroes include at most `MAX_UNDERLEVELED` (1) under its level --
Fran's "optionally 1 underleveled hero". The comp draw in `_decide_embark`
then seated ANY eligible heroes, so a roster with four Veterans was sent to
a Veteran quest with four level-0 fodder. The boss path already bound the
seated party (2026-10-10); ordinary quests did not. In the campaign
simulator those raids were 46% of all deaths. Now, when the quest is in
reach, comps seating more than one under-level hero are dropped from the
draw; a green roster's fallback is unchanged. Campaign: success +5 / +4 pp
on both seed blocks (CAMPAIGN_SIM.md E12). Test:
`test_a_veteran_party_is_seated_with_at_most_one_underlevelled`. This
changes the live bridge's draw too, deliberately.

**Follow-up (aba4c78).** When no curated comp can meet the rule (the
roster's Veterans are of classes no comp combines), the draw used to fall
back to every comp, four level-0s included; it now keeps the comps seating
the fewest under-level heroes. Stepping down a difficulty instead was tried
and was worse (E13, reverted). Campaign: dead -8.4 / -7.3, success +5 / +4
pp (E14). Test: `test_with_no_comp_at_level_the_fewest_underlevelled_are_seated`.

## 2026-10-11 (night): a hero resting on Death's Door rolled a deathblow every turn

`CombatEngine._advance_to_next_hero_turn` ticks a combatant's DoTs at its
turn start and then called `_check_deaths_door_dot` unconditionally. For a
hero already on Death's Door at 0 HP that check goes to `_handle_deaths_door`,
whose already-on-the-door branch is the deathblow roll -- so a downed hero
with NOTHING ticking rolled 33% (base 1.0 minus the 0.67 resist) at the start
of every turn it took. The game rolls only on damage taken: `Actor::vf50`, the
damage handler, takes the deathblow branch inside its `damage > 0` arm and
lets a hit at or under 0 survive; nothing at turn start calls it without a
DoT. The check now runs only when the ticks lowered HP, or for a hero at
<= 0 HP not yet on the door.

Repro, before / after: four Crusaders on the door at 0 HP, the enemy
stunned, one advance -- 17 of 160 dead without a point of damage, then 0
(`tests/engine/test_deaths_door_rest.py`, with a bleeding control that still
rolls).

**Found by the campaign simulator.** In its first 9 weeks, 180 Death's Door
entries ended in 90 deaths (0.83 dead per Apprentice raid), against Fran's
recorded profile_1: 9 entries in ~37 Apprentice fights, 0 dead. The entry
RATE agreed (0.33 vs 0.24 per fight), the gear agreed (his heroes at the
base rank too) and the policy heals a downed ally 33 times in 38 when it can
-- so the gap was the conversion, which is this roll.

`test_hero_death_does_not_end_the_episode` leaned on it: its 1 HP,
0-resist party died at its own turn starts. It now starts on the door, so
the first real hit kills. Every checkpoint trained in the harsher world;
campaign and eval numbers before this commit are in it too.

## 2026-10-11: a curio's loot draws are COUNTS, not weights

`bridge.lootplan._loot_of` priced a Loot outcome as the weighted mean of its
tables. In `curio_type_library.csv` the number after each table of a Loot row
is a **draw count**: the column is labelled `<- # Draws` on 84 of the 87 Loot
rows, and a Loot row carries no percentage per result. So a bare-handed
`locked_strongbox` is `A` twice, the key makes it three times, and
`discarded_pack` is `S` once and `B` twice and a journal page, all of them.
The normalisation made every curio pay ONE draw. (The repo already had the
fact for one curio: 2026-08's "2 draws of *Someone left valuables inside the
tent*".)

Found by calibrating the campaign simulator's income against Fran's own
raids (the recorder's saves, profile_1, 9 Apprentice raids): his packs came
home with 57.5k gold + gems against 33.4k predicted (0.58x), and the curios
investigated paid ~280 each against ~80. After the fix: 40.3k (0.70x); a
map's curios price about twice what they did. The rest of the gap is open:
the model still takes every curio bare-handed and the battle/curio split of
the recorder's snapshots is ambiguous for ~25k of the 57k.

**This moves live decisions too**: every curio value `lootplan` weighs
(deferral to the dark, item assignment, the pack) is now about two to three
times what it was, and an item that adds a draw is worth that draw. Pinned in
`tests/bridge/test_lootplan.py::test_a_loot_row_draws_each_table_its_count_of_times`
(red without the fix).

**And the simulator now uses the basket on the curios** (`campaign.loot`,
10544a2): a curio takes the shop item that beats bare hands on it
(`provision_needs._item_wins`, the same rule the basket is derived from)
while the raid's basket holds one, shovels for the hallway obstacles first.
With both changes the modelled pack is 0.97x Fran's (55.7k vs 57.5k over his
9 recorded raids); `docs/CAMPAIGN_SIM.md` E8.

## 2026-10-10 (evening): monster skills at `.dmg 0 0` deal no damage

Written on the 2026-10-06 night and carried uncommitted through every F1/F2
campaign measurement (both arms alike, so those A/Bs stand). 182 `.dmg 0 0`
entries ship in the install's `.info.darkest` files: stress attacks, marks,
curses, guards, summons. Three readings of them were wrong:

- **The loader read the zero band as "absent"** (`dmg_min if dmg_min else
  None`), so the executor kept the band the monster's PREVIOUS skill left
  behind: the Prophet's mark hit for his 21-42 rubble. `data/hero_loader.py`
  now loads a two-number `.dmg` as a real range, zero included.
- **`resolve_attack` floored every hit at 1**, so a scream on a hero at
  Death's Door rolled a deathblow. A monster at a zero band now deals 0; the
  hit still lands, so its effects and stress apply, and it spends no Aegis
  Block ("non-zero direct damage").
- **The band a zero skill zeroes is restored afterwards**
  (`_monster_ai._execute_enemy_skill`), because `enemy.dmg_min/max` is also
  what the observation and the threat forecast show as the monster's attack;
  the opening band of a monster whose first skill is `.dmg 0 0` stays 1-1.
  So no checkpoint's observation changes.

Evidence: the shipped band itself. The code comment also cites the game's
debug trace logging no damage draw for such a skill; that capture was not
written up separately, so treat it as unconfirmed until a hit-oracle run
records it. Pinned in `tests/engine/test_zero_damage_skills.py` (Madman's
doomsay: no HP lost over 20 seeds, stress still lands, band restored; red
without the fix); `test_monster_heal_field_now_parses` now expects the
`(0, 0)` band.

## 2026-10-05 (overnight): the dice are known -- RNG prediction, fate look-ahead, progression

**Why.** Fran, leaving it running overnight: *"aplica conceptos y métricas
imposibles para humanos para optimizar las jugadas, como si tienes que simular
el RNG ... queremos que una IA juegue a darkest dungeon mucho mejor que yo"*.

**The finding (DECOMP.md F15).** The battle RNG is Park-Miller minstd, and the
save's battle seed is the state at the start of the hero's turn (67/67). The
hit roll is draw 2 (50/50, and 26/26 in an August trace), the first target's
damage draw 5 (62/62 values), its crit draw 6. So at every hero decision the
bridge KNOWS whether each option hits, crits, and for how much.

**Built.**
- `engine/game_rng.py`: the generator, exact on traced draws.
- `CombatEngine.fate` and `resolve_attack(crit_roll=, damage_u=)`: the known
  draws used for the hero's attack. Off by default and byte-identical: a
  trajectory-oracle run with and without the hook gives the same hashes. The
  official recording is already stale from earlier uncommitted engine edits;
  that predates this change.
- `bridge/fate.py`: the forecast from the save, an env clone that shares the
  store, `q_values` (r + gamma V(s') from the policy's own critic, common random
  numbers across actions), and `gated_choice`: the policy's pick stands unless
  the known dice make one of its top-4 better by more than a margin.
- `Advisor(fate=True)` / `run.py --fate` / `overnight.py --fate`: every advice
  block prints the turn's rolls, and a veto says what it switched and why.
- `env/fate_wrapper.py` + `train.py --fate-obs`: a policy that SEES the three
  known draws (observation +3) and whose attack uses them -- the same numbers
  the bridge reads from the save. A 12M run of the shipped recipe with it is
  `models/fate_obs_s0.zip`.
- `tools/fate_eval.py`: policy / oracle / gate / fate arms on identical
  `--all-comps` scenarios, paired.

**A negative result kept on purpose.** Searching EVERY legal action with the
exact future scored 43.3% SUCCESS against the policy's 93.3% (n=60 paired,
2 won, 32 lost) with fewer deaths: the critic rates retreat and abandon as
safe ends. The look-ahead no longer proposes retreat, abandon or pass.

**Progression (the 2026-09-28 audit).** `pick_quest` is no longer
shortest-first. It takes the highest difficulty four rested eligible heroes can
field, with at most one of them under-levelled; a boss only for a trinket
`worth_a_boss` and never under-levelled; and the lowest difficulty when nothing
is in reach. `Quest.reward_trinkets` is read from the save. 5 new tests.

**Measured and left alone.** Corridor DoTs (F12): 2.7 ticks per corridor
measured against the env's 3. Torch snuff timing: Fran clicks a median 0.27 s
after CTRL+SHIFT (37 snuffs); the bridge's 0.35 s dwell is not the cause of its
failures.

## 2026-10-04: the whole game decompiled, and three weeks of mismatched addresses

**Why.** Fran: *"Quiero decompilar el juego Darkest Dungeon para continuar con
el proyecto y mejorarlo mucho mas"*. `docs/DECOMP.md` had read the binary one
config key at a time since 2026-08-19; this exports all of it.

**The bug found first.** The installed `Darkest.exe` changed on 2026-09-15
(sha256 `d83a6d57…`, was `e95ff9f0…`). `tools/pe_anchors.py` scans the INSTALL,
so it followed the patch; `tools/ghidra_export.py --key` handed the addresses it
found to the Ghidra program imported from the AUGUST pin. Every `--key` lookup
since the patch asked the old build about the new build's addresses, and
nothing errored. `--key` now hashes both files and refuses when they differ.

**What.**
- The new build is pinned as `D:\ghidra\bin\Darkest_d83a6d57.exe`, imported
  into the same project beside the old one (analysis 331 s), and is the default.
- `tools/ghidra/ApplyRtti.java` names 14,584 functions from the 5,298
  vftables: `Class::vfN`, `Class::ctor_dtor`, and 360 real game functions by
  name and signature from lambda RTTI (`Battle::ApplyHit`,
  `Actor::ApplyDeathsDoor`, `Battle::GetMonsterAISelectSkill`, ...).
- `tools/ghidra/ExportAll.java` (`ghidra_export.py --all`) decompiles every
  function, 55,972 in 276 s on 8 threads, 3 timeouts, with function, call,
  string, class and method indexes.
- `tools/ghidra_diff.py` matches the two builds: 53,309 of 55,932 functions
  (95.3%). Its first two versions matched half that, because the address
  blanking listed prefixes and missed `UNK_`/`_DAT_`, and because 27,486
  functions share a body with another and were never unique. Blanking every
  token that holds an image address and pairing shared bodies in address order
  fixed both. 13 of 53,309 pairs sit off their neighbours' address trend.

**Finding (DECOMP.md F8).** The patch changed no combat rule: `ApplyHit` and
`ApplyMiss` differ only in struct offsets, and the rest of the combat-path
changes are a null check before `strcmp(mode, "arena_mp")` (63 functions), one
new flag byte in `ActorCombatSkill`'s constructor, and a trinket-buff refresh at
raid start. Every code address F1–F7 cite maps to an identical body.
